# Challenge brief

Source: [Flame in Freefall challenge page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/flame-in-freefall/)

## The ask
Build an **interactive, SI-powered (AI-powered), web-based dashboard** that **summarizes, ranks and interprets** combustion science investigations to deliver fire safety insights for human space exploration.

## The problem NASA states
Results in the Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) database are hard to **find**, harder to **compare**, harder still to **interpret**.

## Suggested (not required) capabilities
1. Identify investigations most relevant to fire safety in crewed environments: spacecraft, stations, lunar and Martian bases, pressurized rovers.
2. Extract data that supports predictive modeling of flammability and fire scenarios.
3. Highlight research that informs next-generation fire safety controls and equipment.

## Other points
- **Audience:** professors, graduate and undergraduate students, aerospace researchers and engineers.
- **Attributes to summarize:** objectives; fuels, sample or burner shape and size; test conditions (oxygen concentration, airflow speed); what varied between tests; sensors and measurement types.
- **Show similarities and differences** between investigations.
- **Earth applications welcome:** water-mist extinguishers, smoke detection, firefighter gear.
- **Out of scope:** rocket and propellant fire safety.
- **Known gap:** PSI has no partial-gravity combustion data yet. NASA says microgravity and normal-gravity results could bound intermediate gravity.

## Oxygen facts from the statement
- Spacesuits: 100% oxygen at 4.3 psi. Earth sea level: 14.7 psi.
- Moon Base and pressurized rovers: expected 30% oxygen or more, at 10.2 psi or less. That is over 40% more oxygen than Earth air, so materials are much more flammable.
- Highest oxygen in microgravity tests: ACME CFI-G, up to 85% oxygen flowing from the burner.
- E-FIELD Flames mimicked partial gravity with an electric field.
- Future partial-gravity experiments: LUCI, FM2.
