## 0:04 - Program scientist, physical sciences, NASA Headquarters
- Fire on a mission is especially dangerous: confined space, far from Earth.
- Much of NASA's fire-in-space data is in PSI, "which you'll be using for this challenge".
- In microgravity there is no buoyancy-driven convection, so ignition, flame spread and extinguishment can differ from normal gravity.
- **Why oxygen goes up:** lunar habitats are expected to have higher oxygen levels, which lowers the risk of decompression sickness ("the bends") and allows more frequent activity in spacesuits. This higher oxygen together with partial gravity "has implications for flammability of materials".
- The datasets can inform **fire safety protocols, design of fire suppression systems, and how materials are screened** for fire risk.

## 1:54 - Subject-matter expert, mechanical and aerospace engineering, UC Irvine
- NASA has studied gaseous, liquid and solid fuels in low gravity under different atmospheres.
- Platforms: drop towers, parabolic flights, sounding rockets, suborbital flights, the ISS.
- Visual examples: candle flame (teardrop and yellow on Earth, spherical and blue in microgravity), E-FIELD Flames (an electric field turns a spherical flame into a teardrop), an acrylic rod burning in fan-driven flow (named as BASS-II), a porous gas burner for flammability and extinction.

## 4:18 - Lead scientist for PSI, NASA Marshall Space Flight Center
- The problem: the volume of data makes a specific investigation or dataset hard to find, and the data hard to interpret for designing safer spacecraft and lunar habitats.
- Combustion Science in PSI: **24 investigations, about 12 terabytes**. Matches our snapshot count (see [[PSI data source]]).
- Each investigation page: overview (objectives, approach) and folders with datasets and documents. In "Data files and documents", tested samples have one folder each.
- Publications marked in blue have a DOI linking to the journal (full text or at least the abstract).

## 7:03 - Subject-matter expert, mechanical and aerospace engineering, Case Western Reserve University
States the ask: an SI-powered web dashboard that summarizes, ranks and interprets combustion investigations. Users should be able to:
1. Identify investigations most relevant to the fire safety needs of habitable crew environments.
2. Extract data that supports predictive modeling of flammability and fire scenarios.
3. Highlight research that informs next-generation fire safety controls and equipment.

Earth add-on: fine water mist extinguishers (originally for the ISS), smoke detection, firefighter gear (protective clothing, air tanks).
